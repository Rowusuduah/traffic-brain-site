# Career Roadmap — EIT to Mastery (Florida traffic)

The trajectory in one line: **reliable producer → trusted analyst → independent problem-solver → PE who owns work → specialist/seller of work.** You can't skip stages; you can shorten them by compounding lessons (that's what this vault is for).

## Stage 0 → 1: Reliable producer (months 0–12)
**Goal:** work that comes back with light redlines. Seniors route tasks to whoever costs them the least review time.
- Master the unglamorous core: counts, Synchro coding, crash pulls, exhibits, comment logs, file hygiene, timesheets.
- Every deliverable through [[QAQC System]] Layer 1 before submission. Track redline density per deliverable — it should visibly fall.
- Learn to read plans even if you don't produce them: [[SPM Plan Review Checklist]], [[OpenRoads and MicroStation File Review]].
- Milestones: own a full TIA analysis chain under supervision; run a parking occupancy study end-to-end; first comment-response matrix handled solo.

## Stage 1 → 2: Trusted analyst (years 1–2.5)
**Goal:** hand you a fuzzy problem, get back a defensible answer with documented assumptions.
- Depth in 2 domains (pick from: operations/TIA, safety, signals, parking, design support) + breadth in the rest.
- Start client-facing reps: meeting notes → sections of reports → speaking to one agenda item.
- Build your automation edge ([[MOC - Automation]]) — the EIT who turns a 6-hour data task into 20 minutes gets pulled into better work.
- **PE exam**: Florida allows taking the PE exam before the 4 years of experience are complete — take Civil: Transportation early while school knowledge is fresh. `#status/verify` current FBPE rules (Ch. 471 F.S. / 61G15 FAC) and NCEES process at fbpe.org.
- Milestones: methodology memo drafted solo; signal timing review with findings a PE accepts; first internal "how-to" you teach someone else.

## Stage 2 → 3: Independent problem-solver (years 2.5–4)
- Scope small studies yourself: study area, data plan, level-of-effort hours.
- QC others' work; own agency coordination threads; present at ITE/FLPRITE district meetings.
- Log every project in [[Experience Tracker for PE-PTOE]] with the *engineering decisions* you made — the PE application asks for exactly this.

## Stage 3 → 4: PE (~year 4) and beyond
- Licensure = liability + responsible charge, not a finish line. Revisit [[EIT Boundaries Ethics and Liability]] from the other side.
- Then choose an edge: **PTOE** (operations credibility), **RSP** (safety practice), design plans production, or modeling — see [[PE PTOE RSP Roadmaps]].
- Years 4–7: run projects, mentor EITs, develop a client relationship of your own. Mastery in consulting = technical depth × judgment × people trust you.

## The compounding habits that separate the top decile
1. Written lessons after every redline round ([[Lessons Learned System]]).
2. Questions asked with homework attached ([[Questions for Senior Engineers]]).
3. A personal library of worked examples — this vault.
4. One skill per month, proven by an artifact ([[Monthly Technical Growth Review]]).
5. Visible reliability: deadlines pre-negotiated, never silently missed.
