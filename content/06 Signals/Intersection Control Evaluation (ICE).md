A warrant analysis answers "could a signal be justified?" — ICE answers the better question: **"which control performs best here?"** FDOT requires an ICE when intersection control decisions are on the table (new signals, control changes, major intersection projects) — scope and applicability per the current **FDOT Manual on Intersection Control Evaluation** (released annually via TEO bulletin; 2026 edition is Bulletin 25-02) `#status/verify applicability triggers against the current ICE Manual and TEM`.

## What it is

A documented, staged comparison of intersection control alternatives (signal, roundabout, RCUT/median U-turn, other alternative intersections, stop control) on safety, operations, cost, and impacts — so the selected control is defended by performance, not habit. Expect the reviewer to ask for the ICE form before discussing your signal recommendation.

## Stages

- **Stage 1 — Screening:** planning-level pass over all viable alternatives; kill the non-starters cheaply. This is where CAP-X (v/c) and SPICE (safety) live.
- **Stage 2 — Alternative selection:** refined operational analysis (HCM/Synchro-level), site-calibrated SPICE crash prediction, cost via FDOT's economic tool (FEAT), leading to a preferred control.
- **Stage 3 — Supplemental:** only if Stage 2 can't isolate a single preferred strategy — deeper cost/impact/stakeholder analysis. `#status/verify stage names and triggers against the current ICE Manual`

## Roundabout-first culture

The practical effect of ICE in Florida: **roundabouts must be given genuine first consideration**, and "we didn't look at a roundabout" is no longer an acceptable study posture `#status/verify exact policy language in the current ICE Manual`. Know the counterarguments that actually hold (ROW, ped volumes, freight geometry, arterial progression) and the ones that don't ("drivers here aren't used to them"). Design/operations reference for the roundabout alternative: **NCHRP Research Report 1043, Guide for Roundabouts** (2023, supersedes NCHRP 672) — free PDF at nap.nationalacademies.org (catalog 27069), covering performance-based planning, operational analysis, and design including mini and compact roundabouts.

## The free screening tools

- **CAP-X** (Capacity Analysis for Planning of Junctions): spreadsheet v/c screening of conventional and alternative intersections/roundabouts from turning movement volumes — hours, not weeks, per alternative.
- **SPICE** (Safety Performance for ICE): spreadsheet comparison of predicted crash performance (SPF/CMF-based) across control types.
- FDOT posts its own maintained versions of both, plus **FEAT** (economic analysis), on the FDOT ICE page (link above).

> [!tip]
> The FHWA ICE page — highways.dot.gov/safety/intersection-safety/ice — hosts the national CAP-X and SPICE downloads plus the ICE Primer; use it when you need the tools outside an FDOT context or want the methodology background.

> [!warning]
> Screening tools are for *comparing* alternatives, not for final design — a CAP-X v/c is not an HCM LOS, and a Stage 1 SPICE run is not an HSM analysis. Say which stage your numbers come from.
