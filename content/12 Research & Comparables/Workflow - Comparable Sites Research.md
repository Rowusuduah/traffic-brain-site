---
**Trigger:** "has anyone done this before?" — calming device precedent, parking reduction defense, treatment effectiveness, agency-acceptance evidence.

1. **Define comparison criteria FIRST** (or you'll cherry-pick): roadway type · lanes · posted speed · area/land-use context · volume band · crash pattern/safety issue · treatment sought · parking context if relevant · bike/ped & school/residential context · agency (same-state ≫ national for acceptance arguments).
2. **Hunt, in order:** target-agency project pages & council/committee agenda packets (goldmine — staff reports attach studies) → neighboring FL agencies' programs → FDOT district studies → national (FHWA case studies, ITE library) → aerial sweep (Google Earth along candidate corridors; historical imagery shows before/after) → your own firm's archive.
3. **Document each hit** in a comparable-site note: its fields ARE the checklist — especially *why comparable* (criteria matrix vs your site) and *before/after if published* (never invent; "not published" is a valid entry).
4. **Use in deliverables:** a comp-sites summary table (site | agency | context | treatment | outcome | source) + 1–2 map/photo exhibits; language: "similar context implemented at [site] by [agency] ([source])" — comparables show precedent/feasibility; CMFs show effectiveness ([[Countermeasure Selection and CMFs]]) — don't ask comps to do CMF work.
5. **Bank it:** every comp note is permanent inventory — the library compounds across projects (Dataview table by treatment = instant retrieval).
**Free companions:** for dating when a treatment was actually built, [FDOT APLUS](https://aplus.fdot.gov/) ([program page](https://www.fdot.gov/gis/aerialmain.shtm)) — Florida's largest historic aerial archive, 1950–2022, free Internet Subscriber Account required · [UF Digital Collections aerials](https://ufdc.ufl.edu/collections/aerials) — no-login pre-1990s coverage. Both reach decades further back than Google Earth's historical slider.
**Example application:** your Miami traffic-calming corridor inventory was industrial-scale step 2+3 — the aerial-tracing workflow generalizes to any device hunt.
**Practice question:** client wants speed cushions on a 6,000 ADT county collector; agency has no program — which THREE criteria matter most in choosing comps that will persuade this agency?
**QA/QC reminder:** every comp cites a source link + last-checked date; a comp you can't source is a rumor.
