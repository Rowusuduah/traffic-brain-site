This is the **Traffic Engineering Brain** — a working operating system for going from entry-level EIT to a strong Florida traffic engineer. Part of it arrives pre-filled: core references, workflows, and worked examples. The rest is deliberately scaffolded, because notes you write yourself are the ones you retrieve under pressure; the system's job is to make writing them take 90 seconds, not to hand you a textbook you'll never reread.

## The operating loop
The Brain runs on a simple rhythm anyone can adopt:
- **Capture** (all day): anything worth keeping goes into an inbox or a daily log. Don't file, don't format. See [[Daily Capture Routine]].
- **Weekly** (Friday, 20 min): [[Weekly Review Routine]] — empty the inbox into real notes, log lessons, queue questions for senior engineers.
- **Monthly** (last Friday, 45 min): [[Monthly Technical Growth Review]] — take honest stock of skills, pick next month's focus.
- **Per project**: open a project note on day one; close it out with written lessons learned.

## Where everything lives
Content is organized in numbered folders, 00–22. Each numbered folder now opens with a curated hub page — click the folder itself and you land on it — indexing what's built and what's planned; the old separate `MOC - ` notes have folded into these hubs (old links still resolve). `00 System` runs the machine — routines, QA/QC, lessons learned. `02 References` holds the core manuals (FDM, Standard Plans, MUTCD, Greenbook, HCM, HSM, and the rest), each with what/why/when and links. `03 Agencies` is the Florida jurisdiction library. Folders `04` onward are knowledge domains — safety and calming, parking, signals, TIA, design and CAD, software, automation, data sources, research, reports, PM, AI, and career; start with the hub when entering a domain. The remaining numbered folders — templates (sheet `01` included), projects, daily logs, inbox — are private working space that lives in the private vault and isn't published here. [[How This Vault Works]] covers the naming, tagging, and linking conventions. Worked examples are marked ★ throughout.

## Rules of the vault
1. **Verify before you rely.** Anything tagged `#status/verify` (statute numbers, manual editions, URLs) gets checked against the current official source before it touches real work. Log the date in the note's `last-checked` field.
2. **Official sources decide; everything else teaches.** Source classes 1–2 (official/professional) for engineering judgment; classes 3–7 (tutorials, examples, Reddit, GitHub, opinion) for learning only. See [[Workflow - Research and Source Vetting]].
3. **An EIT does not sign, seal, or make final engineering decisions.** When in doubt → [[EIT Boundaries Ethics and Liability]] → ask the PE.
4. Links rot. Every reference note has a `last-checked` date. [[Standards Update Tracker]] tells you what to re-check quarterly.
